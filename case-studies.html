Case Study: Player A — Improving Load Sequence and Front-Side Stability

A baseline hitting case focused on back-hip load, swing sequence, contact consistency, and finish control.

22 / 30
Initial Score

Player Profile

Age: Youth player
Level: Youth Baseball
Focus Area: Back-hip load, front-side support, and swing sequence

Video Angles Used

Side Tee + Back Tee + Side Toss + Back Toss

Initial Observation

Player A showed good swing intent and a trainable foundation. The main improvement needs were a more complete back-hip load, less early hand and upper-body involvement, stronger front-side support, and a more stable finish direction.

Key Findings

  • Back-hip load was not always complete.
  • Hands and upper body sometimes started early.
  • Front-side support needed to become more stable.
  • Finish direction and balance were not fully repeatable.

Vincent’s Recommendations

  • Build a stronger back-hip load before launch.
  • Let the body lead before the hands release.
  • Improve front-side support after stride.
  • Hold the finish to check balance and direction.

Training Drills

  • Load Pause Drill
  • Stride-and-Hold Drill
  • Connection Drill
  • Hit-and-Freeze

Follow-Up Goal

Increase high-quality contact, reduce poor contact, and improve body sequence and finish stability during the next retest.

Case Study: Player B — Shortening Swing Path and Improving Direction

A baseline hitting case focused on swing path, timing, front-side direction, and contact depth.

23 / 30
Initial Score

Player Profile

Age: Youth player
Level: Youth Baseball
Focus Area: Swing path, hand timing, and front-side direction

Video Angles Used

Side Tee + Back Tee + Side Toss + Back Toss

Initial Observation

Player B showed clear strength, good swing intent, and acceptable contact. The main improvement need was not raw strength, but a shorter swing path, better sequencing, and more stable front-side direction.

Key Findings

  • Swing path could get long or too big before contact.
  • Hands and upper body sometimes started too early.
  • Front side opened early enough to affect direction and stability.
  • Contact depth and sweet-spot consistency fluctuated.

Vincent’s Recommendations

  • Shorten the path into the contact zone.
  • Keep energy in the back hip and back leg before launch.
  • Train body-first rotation with later hand release.
  • Use finish control to check body direction.

Training Drills

  • Short-to-Contact Drill
  • Load Pause Drill
  • Stride-and-Hold Drill
  • Hit-and-Freeze

Follow-Up Goal

Increase high-quality contact by shortening the swing path, improving front-side stability, and making contact depth more repeatable.

Case Study: Player C — Controlling Forward Move and Contact Depth

A baseline hitting case focused on forward-move control, front-side block, timing consistency, and finish balance.

24 / 30
Initial Score

Player Profile

Age: Youth player
Level: Youth Baseball
Focus Area: Forward move, front-side block, and contact depth

Video Angles Used

Side Tee + Back Tee + Side Toss + Back Toss

Initial Observation

Player C showed strong body coordination, good swing intent, clear lower-half involvement, and a complete finish. The main development need was control: the forward move could get too large, the front side could open early, and contact depth needed to become more repeatable.

Key Findings

  • Forward move sometimes became too large before contact.
  • Front side opened early enough to affect direction and stability.
  • Contact depth and timing consistency fluctuated.
  • Finish balance was not fully repeatable on some swings.

Vincent’s Recommendations

  • Control the forward move after the leg lift.
  • Build a stronger front-side block.
  • Keep the front shoulder and front hip from opening too early.
  • Hold the finish to check body direction and balance.

Training Drills

  • Controlled Leg Lift Drill
  • Stride-and-Hold Drill
  • Hit Behind the Front Side
  • Hit-and-Freeze

Follow-Up Goal

Increase the percentage of good contact by controlling forward move, stabilizing the front side, and making contact depth more consistent.

Development Process

How to Read These Case Studies

Each case study begins with a baseline observation, then turns the video findings into a small set of practical training priorities. The goal is not to overwhelm the player, but to focus on the changes that can create better contact and more repeatable mechanics.

Future retests can compare the same video angles, contact quality score, and issue frequency to show whether the player is improving over time.

What Each Report Includes

  • Player profile and focus area
  • Initial swing observation
  • Video angles used
  • Contact quality score
  • Key mechanical findings
  • Vincent’s recommendations
  • Training drills
  • Follow-up goal
